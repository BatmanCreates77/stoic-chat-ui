ScreenMeter Snapshot

Measure a monitor's gamma, grey tracking and colour gamut with a single photo from your phone. The chart carries dither patches of exactly known brightness, so the page can undo the camera's tone curve without controlling the camera.

On the MacBook

  1. Set brightness to the level you normally use. Turn off True Tone, Night Shift and auto-brightness. Pick the sRGB or default colour profile in System Settings.
  2. Make sure the display scaling is an integer step. The chart reports the pixel ratio; it must be exactly 1 or 2.
  3. Darken the room. Glossy screens reflect everything behind you into the photo.
  4. Open the chart. It takes the whole screen; press Esc to come back.

On the S24 Ultra

  1. Open this same link on the phone and tap the button below.
  2. Best: open the Samsung Camera in Pro mode first. ISO 50, shutter around 1/60, white balance fixed, and turn the focus ring slightly off so the screen's pixel grid blurs. Take the shot, then use Choose from gallery here. Quick alternative: Take photo opens the normal camera; turn Scene optimizer and HDR off and keep JPEG (not HEIF).
  3. Stand 30 to 50 cm back, square to the screen, so the chart fills most of the frame. No zoom, no flash. The white patch should look bright but not blown out.
  4. After the photo, drag the four handles onto the four corner markers, then analyse.

What a single photo can and cannot tell you

Reliable: gamma curve shape, grey-step linearity, relative tint drift across greys, approximate gamut triangle.

Not measurable this way: absolute brightness in nits, white point (the camera's auto white balance neutralises it), deep black and contrast (JPEG black crush), flicker. The companion Android app in the repository handles those with RAW capture and locked exposure.

Measure

Drag each handle onto the centre of a corner marker (the small black square inside the white square). The loupe helps with precision.

Results

Gamma curve

Camera tone curve recovered from dither patches

Each dot is a patch whose true brightness is known by construction (1, ½, ¼ … 1/64 of white). The curve is what the camera does to light; it is inverted, pixel by pixel, before any other patch is read. When the photo resolves the dots, the dots sit off the curve by design and a power-law fit is used instead.

Grey tracking

Red minus blue channel balance of each grey step, relative to the white patch. A tilt means the panel's greys lean warm or cool. Auto white balance removes any overall cast, so only the slope is meaningful.

Chromaticity (CIE 1931 xy, assuming the camera renders sRGB)
